# Research methodology

## Scope
Swiggy's consumer-facing food-delivery marketplace in India. Adjacent Swiggy businesses are included only when they inform discovery, membership, affordability, fulfilment, or AI-led commerce.

## Evidence hierarchy
1. Official Swiggy announcements, filings, investor materials.
2. Competitor official materials and regulatory/public filings.
3. Reputable business/industry research.
4. App reviews and community reports (not systematically sampled in this version).

## Evidence labels
- **Fact:** directly stated in a cited public source.
- **Observed behaviour:** directly tested or observed; none is claimed in this version.
- **Inference:** interpretation of one or more sources.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible issue/opportunity requiring validation.
- **Recommendation:** proposed product or business action.
- **Illustrative assumption:** invented input used only to demonstrate a formula.

## Process
1. Define product boundary and key user jobs.
2. Collect official public product announcements and market context.
3. Translate product surfaces into a hypothesised journey, explicitly marking untested friction.
4. Design a measurement framework and event taxonomy.
5. Build an opportunity backlog and prioritise using analyst-estimated RICE inputs.
6. Specify an MVP, technical proposal, experiments, guardrails, and staged roadmap.

## Quality controls
- Do not infer internal Swiggy architecture from external product behaviour.
- Do not treat company-reported performance claims as independently audited unless the source says so.
- Do not use market forecasts as actual company revenue or market share.
- All numeric calculations in the scripts are illustrative.
