# 25 — Executive recommendation

## Recommendation
Prioritise a measured **Meal Confidence** MVP that clarifies total payable cost, ETA uncertainty, trusted menu attributes, and the recovery path—before investing in a broader AI assistant or major redesign.

## 1. Most important user problem
The customer must decide whether an option is suitable across food, cost, and timing, then trust the platform to fulfil the order. The specific level of current friction is unverified and must be measured.

## 2. Why it matters
Food delivery is a high-frequency, multi-sided service. A small improvement in confident choice and successful fulfilment can affect conversion, repeat use, support load, and restaurant/delivery operations. Public Swiggy initiatives show attention to speed, affordability, health-led discovery, and AI-native interactions, but do not by themselves prove the proposed problem.

## 3. Prioritised segment
Time-constrained repeat customers, followed by budget-conscious frequent users. Validate both through research and behavioural data.

## 4. Proposed MVP
- Transparent total payable price.
- ETA range/confidence only when calibrated data exists.
- Verified menu attributes with provenance.
- Clear recovery actions for material service exceptions.
- Event tracking and controlled experiment.

## 5. Success measurement
Primary: successful completed orders per eligible user or checkout completion, chosen before the experiment based on baseline diagnosis. Guardrails: contribution/order, cancellation/refund, ETA error, price surprise, support contacts, order accuracy, restaurant impact, and safety signals.

## 6. What could cause failure
- The hypothesised friction is not material.
- Data sources are stale or not reliable enough.
- Added information creates clutter.
- Better conversion is driven by lower-margin orders.
- ETA confidence is poorly calibrated.
- Dietary attributes imply unsupported certainty.
- Implementation cost exceeds incremental value.

## 7. Scale or stop criteria
Scale only if a pre-registered experiment shows meaningful improvement in the primary metric and no unacceptable guardrail regression. Stop or revise if there is no measurable user benefit, if contribution deteriorates beyond the agreed limit, or if trust/safety issues emerge.

## 8. What not to build yet
Do not start with an autonomous AI ordering agent, a new loyalty programme, or a broad home-screen redesign. First prove the user problem and establish reliable source-of-truth data.

## Final perspective
The strongest product-management decision is not “add AI to Swiggy.” It is to reduce a verified customer uncertainty, measure the complete marketplace effect, and use AI only where it adds demonstrable value beyond clear information design and deterministic systems.
